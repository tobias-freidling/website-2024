### Description

In this weekly course, we read and discuss different papers that examine how
*randomness* is conceptualized in statistics. There are two presentation formats:
(1) We directly examine the reading material and different people can lead the
discussion with
their comments, questions, and opinions; (2) one of the participants starts the 
session with a 20-minute presentation introducing the main points and subsequently
leading the discussion. See the schedule below for details about the topics and
presentations. Regardless of the presentation format, participants enrolled
for the module are expected
to read the material before the session.

Free auditors are always welcome. :)

### Reading Materials

**Randomness, Philosophically**
- Introduction to the main philosophical views on randomness:
 *Hájek, A. (2023) ‘Interpretations of Probability’, in E.N. Zalta and U. Nodelman (eds) The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Winter 2023. Metaphysics Research Lab, Stanford University.*
 [link](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2023/entries/probability-interpret/)

**Randomness, Probabilistically**
- Review of the historical developments leading up to Kolmogorov's axioms:
*Shafer, G. and Vovk, V. (2006) ‘The Sources of Kolmogorov’s Grundbegriffe’, Statistical Science, 21(1), pp. 70–98.*
 [link](https://doi.org/10.1214/088342305000000467)
- Modern retrospective on von Mises' approach to probability: *van Lambalgen, M. (1996) ‘Randomness and foundations of probability: von Mises’ axiomatisation of random sequences’, Statistics, probability and game theory. Institute of Mathematical Statistics, pp. 347–368.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1214/lnms/1215453582)


Optional reading:
- von Mises' formulation of probability: *von Mises, R. (1941) ‘On the Foundations of Probability and Statistics’, The Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 12(2), pp. 191–205.*
 [link](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2235767)

**The Bayesian Perspective**
- Historical Overview: *Savage, L.J. (1961) ‘The Foundations of Statistics Reconsidered’, Proceedings of the Fourth Berkeley Symposium on Mathematical Statistics and Probability, Volume 1: Contributions to the Theory of Statistics. University of California Press, pp. 575–587.*
[link](https://projecteuclid.org/ebooks/berkeley-symposium-on-mathematical-statistics-and-probability/Proceedings-of-the-Fourth-Berkeley-Symposium-on-Mathematical-Statistics-and/chapter/The-Foundations-of-Statistics-Reconsidered/bsmsp/1200512183)
- Modern and more statistical overview of the Bayesian approach: *Gelman, A. and Shalizi, C.R. (2013) ‘Philosophy and the practice of Bayesian statistics’, British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology, 66(1), pp. 8–38*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8317.2011.02037.x)

Optional reading:
- Non-technical exposition of the decision-theoretic approach: *Savage, L.J. (1951) ‘The Theory of Statistical Decision’, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 46(253), pp. 55–67.*
[link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/01621459.1951.10500768)

- de Finetti's contributions: *De Finetti, B. (1992) ‘Foresight: Its Logical Laws, Its Subjective Sources’, in S. Kotz and N.L. Johnson (eds) Breakthroughs in Statistics. New York, NY: Springer New York (Springer Series in Statistics), pp. 134–174.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0919-5_10)


**The Frequentist Perspective**
- An argument for the likelihood principle: *Birnbaum, A. (1962) ‘On the Foundations of Statistical Inference’, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 57(298), pp. 269–306.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1962.10480660)

- Fisherian vs Neyman-Pearsonian point of view: *Lehmann, E.L. (1993) ‘The Fisher, Neyman-Pearson Theories of Testing Hypotheses: One Theory or Two?’, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 88(424), pp. 1242–1249.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1993.10476404)

Optional reading:
- An interpretation of the Neyman-Pearson theory: *Birnbaum, A. (1977) ‘The Neyman-Pearson Theory as Decision Theory, and as Inference Theory; With a Criticism of the Lindley-Savage Argument for Bayesian Theory’, Synthese, 36(1), pp. 19–49.*
[link](https://www.jstor.org/stable/20115212)

- Fisher on statistical errors: *Fisher, R. (1955) ‘Statistical Methods and Scientific Induction’, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Methodological), 17(1), pp. 69–78.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1955.tb00180.x)

- Pearson's response to Fisher's paper: *Pearson, E.S. (1955) ‘Statistical Concepts in the Relation to Reality’, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society. Series B (Methodological), 17(2), pp. 204–207.*
[link](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2983954)


**Finite and Infinite Populations I**
- Fisher's approach to inference (read in excerpts): *Fisher, R.A. (1922) ‘On the mathematical foundations of theoretical statistics’, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London. Series A, Containing Papers of a Mathematical or Physical Character, 222(594–604), pp. 309–368.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1922.0009)
- Retrospective on Fisher and Neyman's contributions on sampling: *Fienberg, S.E. and Tanur, J.M. (1996) ‘Reconsidering the Fundamental Contributions of Fisher and Neyman on Experimentation and Sampling’, International Statistical Review / Revue Internationale de Statistique, 64(3), p. 237.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.2307/1403784)
- Outline of Fisher's idea with discussion (shared with the next session): *Fisher, R.A. (1935) ‘The Logic of Inductive Inference’, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, 98(1), pp. 39–82.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.2307/2342435)

Optional reading:
- Fisher's foundational work on random experiments: *Fisher, R.A. (1935) ‘The design of experiments.’*
[link](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Cahyono-St/publication/361343558_The_Design_Of_Experiments/links/62ab61f440d84c1401af48bd/The-Design-Of-Experiments.pdf)
- A Bayesian perspective on finite populations: *Ericson, W.A. (1969) ‘Subjective Bayesian Models in Sampling Finite Populations’, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Methodological), 31(2), pp. 195–224.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1969.tb00782.x)

**Finite and Infinite Populations II**
- Outline of Fisher's idea with discussion (shared with the previous session): *Fisher, R.A. (1935) ‘The Logic of Inductive Inference’, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, 98(1), pp. 39–82.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.2307/2342435)
- Neyman on sample surveys (read in excerpts): *Neyman, J. (1934) ‘On the Two Different Aspects of the Representative Method: The Method of Stratified Sampling and the Method of Purposive Selection’, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, 97(4), p. 558.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.2307/2342192)
- An early interpretation of census data: *Deming, W.E. and Stephan, F.F. (1941) ‘On the Interpretation of Censuses as Samples’, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 36(213), pp. 45–49.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.2307/2278811)

Optional reading:
- Theoretical paper on finite population inference: *Pitman, E.J.G. (1937) ‘Significance Tests Which May be Applied to Samples from Any Populations’, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology, 4(1), pp. 119–130.*
[link](https://doi.org/10.2307/2984124)
